## What makes a GoHighLevel job cost more?

The price rises with the number of moving parts. A single workflow fix is a small job. A setup with several pipelines, two-way integrations, US texting registration and data moved from another platform takes far longer, because each part has to be built and then tested together.

| Cost driver | Why it adds time |
| --- | --- |
| Number of workflows | Each one needs its triggers, filters and branches built and tested with real contacts |
| Integrations and APIs | Zapier, Make, n8n, webhooks or custom API work add steps outside GHL that can fail |
| Moving data from another tool | Contacts, tags and custom fields have to be cleaned and mapped before import |
| Email and phone setup | A dedicated sending domain and A2P 10DLC registration involve DNS records and carrier review |
| SaaS mode and rebilling | Plans, Stripe, snapshots and usage rebilling all have to work together |
| An account someone else built | Old workflows and duplicate fields have to be understood before anything is changed |

## Should you pay hourly, a fixed price or a monthly retainer?

Pay a fixed price for a job you can describe, such as a setup, a migration or a specific fix. Pay hourly only for small, open-ended tasks, and set a cap. Choose a monthly retainer once the account is live and you expect a steady trickle of changes. Each model puts the risk of overruns on a different side.

| Model | Best for | Who carries the risk if it runs long |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hourly | Small tasks with unclear scope | You |
| Fixed price | Setups, migrations and defined fixes | The expert |
| Monthly retainer | Ongoing changes to a live account | Shared, within the monthly limit |

## How do you check a GoHighLevel expert before you hire them?

Ask to see work similar to yours, agree the scope in writing, and confirm you will own the account. HighLevel also runs a Certified Admin program: its partner page says the training is free, and its November 2025 announcement says admins pass a live practical exam before they are listed in its directory at directory.gohighlevel.com.

1. **Describe the job in writing.** List what should happen, from the first form fill to the booked call.
2. **Ask for a fixed quote or an hour cap.** Get the total before work starts.
3. **Ask to see similar work.** A screen share of a past build tells you more than a profile.
4. **Keep ownership.** The account stays in your name, and the expert works as an invited user.
5. **Agree how it will be tested.** Ask who tests each workflow, and with which test contacts.
6. **Ask for a handover.** A recorded walkthrough means you or your team can run the account afterwards.
7. **Check what happens after launch.** Ask how long fixes to their own work are free.

## What we see in real accounts

When we open an account someone else configured, the contacts are usually the first problem. Many accounts hold thousands of contacts with duplicates among them, and emails keep going out to those duplicates.

Workflows come next. Agency owners and their teams build a lot of workflows, and at some point they start to overlap and conflict. We keep workflows as few and as simple as we can, so anyone on the team can understand them.

We have worked with clients running Facebook ads whose team was not receiving internal notifications, because the workflows behind them were not configured right. Calendars have the same kind of gap: availability, the times leads can book, and which team member takes each lead are often never set up.

User roles cause confusion too. When an admin adds a user without giving them access to the right tools, that user opens the account and cannot find the options they need.
